Summary: Good work often begins before the first decision, in the patient act of noticing what is already there.

Attention is easy to confuse with concentration. Concentration narrows the field until only the task remains. Attention is more generous. It notices the task, the room around it, and the small signal that the original question may have been wrong.

In design, this distinction matters. A concentrated person can polish the wrong surface for hours. An attentive person notices that the surface should not exist.

## Looking before arranging

The most durable interfaces often feel inevitable because they begin with observation rather than decoration. What repeats? What interrupts? Where does a reader hesitate? Which piece of information carries the weight, and which pieces merely announce that a design process occurred?

Removing noise is not the same as making everything quiet. A newspaper uses rules, columns, numbers, and changes in scale. Its restraint comes from assigning each device a job and refusing to let it perform another.

Attention is the discipline behind that refusal. It is slow enough to see the difference between an empty space and a missing idea.
