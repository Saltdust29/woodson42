Summary: Longevity is less about predicting the future than making the present structure easy to understand.
Reading: 3 min read

Software rarely survives because its authors anticipated every future requirement. It survives because the next person can discover what it is doing, why it is doing it, and where a change belongs.

The same is true of physical tools. A well-made object reveals its joints. Its materials behave like themselves. Repair is part of the design rather than evidence that the design has failed.

## Legibility is a feature

There is a temptation to measure sophistication by the number of decisions hidden from view. Sometimes abstraction earns that trust. Sometimes it merely moves the confusion to a place with a better name.

A lasting system has a readable shape: a small number of strong boundaries, explicit inputs and outputs, and conventions that remain boring under pressure. Its deepest modules conceal complexity; its surface tells the truth.

Future-proofing is an impossible promise. Legibility is a practical gift.
